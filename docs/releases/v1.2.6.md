# POM2 v1.2.6

> [!WARNING]
> **First launch on macOS or Windows: you have to allow POM2 once.** The packages
> are not notarized by Apple or signed for Windows, so both systems block a
> downloaded copy.
>
> **🍏 macOS: "Apple could not verify POM2".** Drag **POM2** from the `.dmg` into
> *Applications* and try to open it. Click **Done**, then open the Apple menu →
> **System Settings** → **Privacy & Security**, scroll down to **Security**, click
> **Open Anyway** and confirm.
>
> A package older than v1.1.2 says *"POM2 is damaged"* instead and has no
> **Open Anyway**. Do not move it to the Trash. Run this in Terminal:
>
> ```bash
> xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/POM2.app
> ```
>
> **🪟 Windows: "Windows protected your PC".** Click **More info**, then
> **Run anyway**.

The debugger's second round, from the remarks of a demo coder who uses it
every day; and disks packed in `.7z`.

**Debugger**

- **Three kinds of breakpoint in one list**, named as AppleWin names them:
  **BP** stops when the PC reaches an address, **BPMR** when an address is
  read, **BPMW** when it is written. A memory breakpoint shows the byte
  that is there now. What POM2 called a "watch" was really a BPMR / BPMW.
- **A Watch window that only watches** (Tools menu): addresses and their
  values, live, as a byte, a 16-bit word or in binary; what changed since
  the previous stop is highlighted. It never stops the machine.
- **Right-click an address** — a line of the listing, its operand, a byte
  of the Memory viewer — to set a BP, a BPMR or a BPMW on it, watch it, run
  to it.
- **Conditions are added afterwards**, on the breakpoint's row: a register,
  the byte read or written, the first N passes to let go by — and **the
  position of the beam**: stop on a write to `$C054` only outside horizontal
  blanking. Every breakpoint counts its passes.
- **Step Back and Run Back** (Shift+F7, Ctrl+F7): one instruction back, or
  back to the previous breakpoint. They use the rewind's history: tick
  **Rec** beside the two buttons, then run. POM2 re-executes from a recorded
  frame and checks that it arrives exactly where the machine stood; when it
  cannot (just after a key, a reset, a byte sent to a serial port…) the
  buttons are greyed and say why.

**Screen Inspector**

- **The beam is a cross** over the whole picture, in two tones and a colour
  of your choice: the red line was lost on a hi-res orange. In blanking, an
  arrow on the edge the beam is behind.
- **Run until the beam is there**: right-click the picture; or Next line,
  Next frame, VBL.
- **Switches**: every video soft switch thrown during the frame, with its V
  and H, the byte of the line it takes effect from, and **the instruction
  that threw it**. A click sends the Debugger's listing there.

**Disks**

- **`.7z` archives open like `.zip`**: dropped on the window, on the command
  line, from a URL, in a drive's picker, in the libretro core and in the
  browser. LZMA, LZMA2 and PPMd.
- **An archive's own `.m3u`** decides which disk is mounted and the order of
  the set.

**Also**

- **No more speaker scratches at start-up**: the audio starts wide, then
  comes down once the machine runs steady.
- **Tools menu** in four headed sections; AI control has its own.
- On macOS, the debugger's Ctrl shortcuts answer to the Ctrl key.
- **POM2 in RetroArch, v1.2.6.** The libretro core released with this
  version ([POM2 Libretro v1.2.6](https://github.com/habib256/pom2/releases/tag/libretro-v1.2.6))
  opens `.7z` content.
