# POM2 v1.2.5

> [!WARNING]
> **First launch on macOS or Windows: you have to allow POM2 once.** The packages
> are not notarized by Apple or signed for Windows, so both systems block a
> downloaded copy.
>
> **🍏 macOS: "Apple could not verify POM2".** Drag **POM2** from the `.dmg` into
> *Applications* and try to open it. Click **Done**, then open the Apple menu →
> **System Settings** → **Privacy & Security**, scroll down to **Security**, click
> **Open Anyway** and confirm.
>
> A package older than v1.1.2 says *"POM2 is damaged"* instead and has no
> **Open Anyway**. Do not move it to the Trash. Run this in Terminal:
>
> ```bash
> xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/POM2.app
> ```
>
> **🪟 Windows: "Windows protected your PC".** Click **More info**, then
> **Run anyway**.

A release for people who WRITE Apple II software in POM2. It answers the
requests of Grouik (French Touch), who now develops his productions in it.

- **A debugger you can read.** Addresses, bytes and mnemonics line up in a
  fixed-pitch font. The listing comes first and takes the height; the wheel,
  the arrows and Page Up / Page Down move in it, and "Go to" takes an
  address or a label.
- **AppleWin's keys.** Space steps, Ctrl+Space steps over, Shift+Space
  steps out, Ctrl+Down runs to the cursor, Right follows a target, Tab
  returns to the PC.
- **The machine's own addresses are named, with no symbol file.**
  `LDA $C055` reads "PAGE2 on", `JSR $FDED` reads "COUT", and the registers
  of a Mockingboard or a Disk II are named in the slot they are plugged in.
  Your own symbols still load, and win.
- **Five lines say where the machine is.** CPU, the video switches by
  name, the memory bank read at each region, the beam (V and H, numbered as
  in AppleWin), and a cycle counter with a mark you reset: put a breakpoint
  at each end of a routine, Run, and read what it costs.
- **Slow motion.** The speed menu goes down to 0.1 %. POM2 counts every
  instruction's cycles exactly but advances by whole instructions, so the
  picture moves by instructions, not by cycles.
- **Beam view.** The screen shows what the tube has at this cycle: the
  frame in progress above the beam, the previous one below. Use it when
  stopped in the middle of a frame, or in slow motion.
- **Screen Inspector** (Tools menu). The picture really displayed, with
  the beam on it. An edge on the left gives every line its mode and page
  and marks the lines where a switch is thrown inside; an edge on top reads
  one line byte by byte. Under the mouse: the mode and page of that byte,
  its address and the value the beam read. It saves the PNG, and the video
  pages as the beam fetched them (hi-res, text / lo-res and their aux
  halves), which the Paint editor loads.
- **Layouts that are clean.** Emulation, Mini-Apple II (the screen above
  its keyboard) and Debug (Screen Inspector beside the Debugger). Each
  shows its own panels and closes the others; nothing is left floating.
- **Tidier menus.** Devices opens with Slot Configuration and Drives, then
  one submenu per kind of card; the accelerators and the Premium SoftCard
  moved there from Machine. The debugging panels are in Tools. In File,
  the BASIC listing and the sessions are at the bottom.
- **Bug reports that say what the machine was.** Help ▸ Report a bug now
  fills the issue with the cards, the disks (file names only), the CPU, the
  video switches, the memory map and the GPU, and copies the full report
  to the clipboard.
- **Thank you.** Help ▸ Thank you rolls the names of everyone POM2 stands
  on, like the end of a film. Welcome and About are rewritten.
- **Speaker SFX editor** (Tools menu), from POM1: design a sound effect
  for the Apple II's 1-bit speaker with 50 ready-made cues, hear it, and
  export the assembler table your program plays through `$C030`.
- **One beep at start-up.** POM2 booted the machine twice when it restored
  your profile, which could cut the first beep.
- **"Download missing ROMs" replaces a wrong dump** in the folder POM2
  reads first, instead of failing on it every time; the old file is kept.
- **F8 for full screen.** F8 switches between the window and the kiosk,
  like F10 and Ctrl+Alt+F; Alt+Enter does it with the pointer captured.
  The Debugger's step-over is Ctrl+Space. Every menu item has an icon.

There is no libretro release with this version: the core has nothing new
for a frontend, and [POM2 Libretro v1.2.4](https://github.com/habib256/pom2/releases/tag/libretro-v1.2.4)
stays the current one.
